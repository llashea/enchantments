The usual trip, day by day

Five trips ran four or five nights, all with camps in the Core. Four followed this shape. It needs a Core permit.

Day Route Mapped miles Night
1 Stuart Lake Trailhead to Colchuck Lake, about 2,200 ft up 4.0 Colchuck Lake
2 Up Aasgard Pass with full packs, about 2,300 ft up, into the upper Core 3.1 to Inspiration Lake Inspiration Lake or nearby
3 A short move through the Core 0.6 to Sprite Lake, 1.1 to Leprechaun Lake Sprite or Leprechaun Lake
4 Down to the Snow Lakes or Nada Lake 2.8 from Leprechaun Lake to the Snow Lakes, 3.9 to Nada Lake Snow Lakes or Nada Lake

The five-night trips added a night. One spent two nights at Colchuck Lake before the pass. One moved camp about a mile at a time through the Core and used two half days for Little Annapurna and for Prusik Pass with Gnome Tarn.

No trip of six or seven nights turned up in the reports read.

If you have a Core permit

Your permit. Camp in any zone and start or finish at either trailhead.

Suggested plan. Use the table at the top of this page, then slow it down. Move camp a mile or two at a time and add side trips.

The longer trips in the reports spent extra days on Little Annapurna, Prusik Pass and Gnome Tarn, and one on a rest day at Leprechaun Lake.

Trip reports: Wanderpost, WTA writer Hike PNW, AARshley.

If you have a Colchuck permit

Your permit. Every night at Colchuck Lake or elsewhere in the Colchuck Zone. Day hikes into the Core are allowed. You cannot camp right below Aasgard Pass.

Suggested plan. Camp at Colchuck Lake every night. A different day hike each full day.

If you have a Snow permit

Your permit. Every night at or below Upper Snow Lake. Day hikes into the Core are allowed.

Suggested plan. Upper Snow Lake as base camp, Nada Lake for the last night.

If you have a Stuart permit

Your permit. Every night in the Stuart Zone, around Lake Stuart. Day hikes into other zones are allowed.

Suggested plan. Lake Stuart as base camp. Day hikes to Colchuck Lake, Horseshoe Lake and, for a long day, up Aasgard Pass.

One party went from Lake Stuart up Aasgard and back in about 13 miles and 12 hours.

If you have an Eightmile/Caroline permit

Your permit. Every night in the Eightmile/Caroline Zone. It is a separate trail system from the traverse.

Suggested plan. Eightmile Lake, then Lake Caroline as base camp. Windy Pass and Little Caroline Lake as day hikes.

Every night stays in the Eightmile/Caroline Zone.

Side trips

Among the five longer trips, two named Little Annapurna, three Prusik Pass and three Gnome Tarn. Hike At Your Pace describes side trips. WTA writer tiffanyc describes moving camp in the lower Core. These destinations are off the mapped traverse line. This page gives no route to them.

Recreation.gov's Aasgard Pass caution covers climbing and descending. Read the agency caution.

Check your reserved stay

For the 2026 season, the Forest Service states a maximum stay of 14 consecutive days. Its rule excludes leaving and re-entering at a later date. Recreation.gov says entry must be on the permit's stated date. These facts were checked September 28, 2026. Forest Service permit rules, Recreation.gov conditions.

For the 2026 season, Recreation.gov permits groups of up to eight people. It says days may be added eight days or more before entry. These rules were checked September 28, 2026. Read the reservation conditions.

The agency also limits unattended camp supplies to 48 hours and requires attractive items to be hung in camp. Read those rules. This page supplies no gear prescription.

The free self-issued day-use permit, your overnight permit and your parking pass are three separate things.
