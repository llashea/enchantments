Trips

You have three nights in the Enchantments

Nine reports describe three-night trips. Seven included camps in the Core. One stayed at Colchuck Lake. One camped at Upper Snow Lake and Nada Lake. Your permit zone still governs where you camp, regardless of how many nights you have.

Agency facts checked September 28, 2026, for the 2026 season. Trip reports read September 28, 2026.

The usual trip, day by day

Seven of the nine three-night trips camped in the Core. This is the shape that crossed from Stuart Lake Trailhead to Snow Lakes Trailhead. It needs a Core permit. Three reports.

Day Route Mapped miles Night
1 Stuart Lake Trailhead to Colchuck Lake, about 2,200 ft up 4.0 Colchuck Lake
2 Up Aasgard Pass with full packs, about 2,300 ft up, into the Core 3.1 to Inspiration Lake, 4.2 to Leprechaun Lake Inspiration or Leprechaun Lake
3 Through the Core and down past the Snow Lakes 3.9 from Leprechaun Lake to Nada Lake Nada Lake
4 Nada Lake to Snow Lakes Trailhead 5.8 Home

Two of the three camped at Nada Lake on night 3. The third stayed at Inspiration Lake and called the last day more than 12 miles out in heat. One went over Aasgard on day 1 instead of camping at Colchuck Lake.

Other three-night shapes: Colchuck Lake all three nights with a day trip up Aasgard. The Snow Lakes twice, then Nada Lake. Up and back down Aasgard with full packs. In and out of Snow Lakes Trailhead with two nights at Leprechaun Lake.

If you have a Core permit

Your permit. Camp in any zone and start or finish at either trailhead.

Suggested plan. Use the table at the top of this page. Colchuck Lake, a night in the Core, Nada Lake, out.

Another way: two nights in the Core and walk out the last day, 9.7 to 10.8 miles. One party did this and called the last day long and hot.

Trip reports: WTA writer clandry, gemhikes, Alec Sills-Trausch.

If you have a Colchuck permit

Your permit. Every night at Colchuck Lake or elsewhere in the Colchuck Zone. Day hikes into the Core are allowed. You cannot camp right below Aasgard Pass.

Suggested plan. Camp at Colchuck Lake all three nights. Spend two days up in the Core with day packs.

If you have a Snow permit

Your permit. Every night at or below Upper Snow Lake. Day hikes into the Core are allowed.

If you have a Stuart permit

Your permit. Every night in the Stuart Zone, around Lake Stuart. Day hikes into other zones are allowed.

Suggested plan. Camp at Lake Stuart all three nights. One day hike each full day.

If you have an Eightmile/Caroline permit

Your permit. Every night in the Eightmile/Caroline Zone. It is a separate trail system from the traverse.

Suggested plan. One night at Eightmile Lake, two at Lake Caroline.

Before you go

Recreation.gov says “Use extreme caution when climbing or descending steep snow slopes, exposed and hidden creeks and waterfalls are present.” This is the agency's Aasgard Pass caution. Read it in full. Neither agency chooses a traverse direction for you.

The Forest Service says “Camp only at previously impacted sites.” Read the permit rules. A camp named in a report does not establish its status.

The free self-issued day-use permit, your overnight permit and your parking pass are three separate things.

See trailheads by zone and the map. Compare one night with two nights, or read four to seven nights. See packing and the app. Road closures and the drive in are on the roads page.
