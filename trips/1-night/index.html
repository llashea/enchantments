You have one night in the Enchantments

Seven trips in this sample lasted one night. Three went into Eightmile/Caroline. One camped at Colchuck Lake and entered the Core for a day hike. Three walked from one trailhead to the other. In all three through trips, the second day continued into the evening.

Three more reports went trailhead to trailhead in one night. All three ran long. One made an unplanned camp at the foot of Aasgard and finished near 10 p.m. One reached the Colchuck boulders at dusk. One took far longer than planned coming down from Lake Viviane.

If you have a Core permit

No one-night report in this sample describes a camp in the Core. That does not establish whether one night fits your trip.

Recreation.gov says “Core Enchantment permit holders may camp in any zone.” The agency describes approaches from Stuart Lake Trailhead and Snow Lakes Trailhead. Read the permit page.

The full traverse is about 17.9 mapped miles. The mapped lines read short. A trailhead-to-trailhead report is not evidence that every permit zone permits that trip.

If you have a Colchuck permit

Skye Stoury's one-night account describes a camp at Colchuck Lake and a same-day walk into the upper Core with day packs. The party returned over Aasgard Pass.

That is a reported trip shape. Recreation.gov's separate rule permits day hiking in the Core but requires camping within the Colchuck Zone. It excludes camping immediately below Aasgard Pass with a Colchuck permit. Read the zone text.

Colchuck Lake is about 4.0 mapped miles from Stuart Lake Trailhead. That distance is one way along this project's line.

If you have a Snow permit

The one-night reports include parties walking through rather than returning to their starting trailhead. WTA writer Abhigdeal describes starting at Snow Lakes Trailhead and camping at Snow Lake before continuing. The report does not state the permit zone.

Your permit rule is separate from that account. Recreation.gov says Snow permit holders may day hike in the Core but must camp at or below Upper Snow Lake. Read the zone text. This page does not establish permission to exit at the other trailhead with a Snow permit.

Snow Lakes are about 6.9 mapped miles from Snow Lakes Trailhead. Nada Lake is about 5.8 mapped miles in.

If you have a Stuart permit

The sample contains no one-night Stuart-camping trip. It cannot supply a night-by-night example for your permit.

Recreation.gov requires Stuart permit holders to camp within the Stuart Zone. Read the zone text. The Forest Service's Stuart Lake Trail page gives 4.5 miles to the lake from Stuart Lake Trailhead. This project has no mapped line to Stuart Lake.

If you have an Eightmile/Caroline permit

All three reported trips started and ended at Eightmile Trailhead. Their camps were at Lake Caroline or Eightmile Lake. All three visited Windy Pass. Savvy Exploring and zilcsak.com describe examples.

Recreation.gov says groups holding this permit may camp only in that zone. Read the zone text. The Eightmile lines share no trail with the traverse. Eightmile Lake is about 3.6 mapped miles from Eightmile Trailhead. Lake Caroline is about 4.8. None of these three trips reached the Core.

Keep the report and the rule separate

The agencies do not name a traverse direction. Recreation.gov's Aasgard Pass caution covers climbing and descending. “Use extreme caution when climbing or descending steep snow slopes, exposed and hidden creeks and waterfalls are present.” Read the full caution.

The free self-issued day-use permit, your overnight permit and your parking pass are three separate things. Entry must be on your permit's stated date. Read the permit rules.

See trailheads by zone and the map. Zone edges are mapped estimates, not campsite rulings. Compare two nights, three nights or four to seven nights. See packing and the app's offline map. Road closures and the drive in are on the roads page.
